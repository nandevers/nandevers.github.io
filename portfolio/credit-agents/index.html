Synthetic interactive demo · agent permissions

A permission is a decision too.

A small synthetic exercise: can an agent complete its task without getting more authority than the task requires?

Illustrative only. Fixed rules and invented records; no deployed agent, model call, or identity-provider test.

What the comparison shows

Task-scoped permissions allow the assigned read and block requests outside the grant, after expiry, or after revocation.

Task + owner→Scoped grant→Policy check→Allow or deny + audit record

The point of the exercise

Make the boundary testable.

Model behavior can be uncertain. Permission should be enforced outside the model so a persuasive instruction cannot grant new authority. A small test suite can make the expected boundary visible to product, engineering, and risk teams.

  • Give the task a distinct identity and a named owner.
  • Limit access by resource, operation, purpose, and time.
  • Test denied, expired, and revoked access—not only the happy path.
  • Keep the attempted action and policy decision in the audit trail.

Personal research illustration. Fixed rules only; no model call, customer data, external service, or production evaluation. Broad access intentionally demonstrates an unsafe configuration.
